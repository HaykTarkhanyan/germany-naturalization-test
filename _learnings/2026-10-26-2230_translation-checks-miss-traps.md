# Script checks passed a translation that taught a wrong exam option; only a reading review caught it

**Symptom:** All 386 Russian lesson texts and 310 Russian question translations passed the checker (same numbers,
same number of bold terms, Cyrillic everywhere, no dashes or curly quotes). An independent reviewer then found that
Q37 and Q309 translated the correct answer "Ministerpräsident(in)" as "премьер-министр". One of the wrong options
of both questions is "Premierministerin/Premierminister": a Russian reader memorising "премьер-министр" would pick
exactly that wrong option on the German sheet.

**Cause:** A natural translation can coincide with a distractor's wording. Mechanical checks see structure, not
meaning; nothing compared a translation with the question's wrong options.

**Also found by the same review:** "**Executive**" in the English lesson source where the exam (Q63) and the lesson's
own trap say "Exekutive"; the translation copied it. And the Words page glossed Ministerpräsident as "state
premier", the same trap in English. All fixed ("министр-президент", "Exekutive", "Minister-President").

**Consequences:** For study text that will be memorised, a mechanical check is necessary but not sufficient: have a
fresh-context reviewer read translation against source, and make the review compare each answer with the
question's wrong options. Keep the exam's own term (German, or the catalog's English) when a plain translation sits
close to a distractor.
