## 24. "My mistakes" means questions whose last answer was wrong

- **Date / status:** 2026-10-26, active (Hayk asked for useful features; mistakes coming back was the biggest gap)
- **Why:** Before, a mistake came back only inside its own topic ("not known yet") or as the one-off "practice the mistakes" of a single exam. The new round (Home, Topics, `#/practice/mistakes/all`) collects every question whose most recent answer, in practice or a mock exam, was wrong. It is a list of real misses, small enough to do in one go, and a question leaves it as soon as it is answered right once.
- **Alternatives rejected:** every "learning" question (answered but not yet right twice in a row): that also pulls in questions answered right once, so the round would mix misses with lucky first tries; a spaced-repetition schedule (due dates per question): more machinery than a 310-question pool needs, and the "known = last two right" rule already decides when a question is learned.
- **What would change this:** The list growing past ~50 for a long time (then cap a round at 20 and start with Bayern questions), or Hayk wanting the second right answer to be required before a question leaves the list.

## 23. The explainer videos are served by the GitHub Pages site, next to their texts

- **Date / status:** 2026-10-26, active (Hayk: "think about UX and what other useful features can be added")
- **Why:** The 28 MP4s were in the repo but not on the site, so they could not be watched on the phone. `app/vite.config.ts` now copies `videos/mp4/` into the build (and streams it in dev, with byte ranges for seeking), and each video page plays its video in the language chosen there; the build fails if a script has no video in either language. - **Alternatives rejected:** linking to the files on github.com (the GitHub file viewer is not a comfortable player on a phone, and raw file URLs are not meant as a video host); YouTube (another account and service, and an outside page to maintain); a GitHub Release (no in-app player, a second place to update after every re-render).
- **What would change this:** The site approaching 1 GB (each re-render replaces the files, it does not add to the site), traffic warnings from GitHub, or deploys getting slow (then move the videos to Release assets or a CDN and keep only the links).

## 18. Vocabulary: spaCy lemmas with hand-checked name filtering, English and articles by Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite

- **Date / status:** 2026-10-11, active, revisited 2026-10-12 (see Revision) (Hayk asked how many German words the catalog has, then for a vocabulary page in the app)
- **Why:** "How many words will I learn" needs dictionary forms, not surface forms (German inflects: wählt, gewählt -> wählen), so `scripts/build_vocabulary.py` lemmatizes all questions and options with spaCy `de_core_news_md` 3.8.0 (spaCy 3.8.16, pinned inline in the script). Names and places (people, cities, countries, states) and all-caps abbreviations are not vocabulary. The model's name detection was wrong for about 70 ordinary words (Staat, Stadt, Frau, Synagoge, Landtag, Bundesverfassungsgericht ...), fixed with a hand-checked list in the script; place-derived adjectives (Warschauer Pakt, Schengener Abkommen) count as names. Feminine forms are merged into the base word (Bürgerin -> Bürger: the catalog always prints both), and one form under two word types (haben as verb and auxiliary) is one word. Result: 1,409 words (823 nouns, 239 verbs, 133 adjectives, 125 adverbs, 85 function words, 4 other), 870 in questions or correct answers; 150 names/places and 14 abbreviations excluded; the 43 most frequent words make up half the text, the top 345 make up 80%. Meanings: `scripts/translate_vocabulary.py` asks Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite on Vertex (the metric account, DECISIONS #17) for the dictionary form with der/die/das and a short English meaning, with one exam sentence as context; 29 calls, $0.073; 40 random words spot-checked, one error fixed by hand (schlagen: "to hit", the "to propose" came from vorschlagen, which spaCy splits).
- **Alternatives rejected:** counting surface forms (1,785: overstates what there is to learn); a dictionary dataset for meanings (no context, many civic compounds missing); translating by hand (slow for 1,409 words; the machine output is cached in the repo and hand fixes stay because reruns keep existing entries); splitting compounds (Bundestagswahl = Bundestag + Wahl, about 150 such nouns): kept as words, the page says how they relate.
- **What would change this:** More translation errors found while studying (fix them in data/vocabulary_en_overrides.json), or a better lemmatizer for separable verbs.
- **Revision (2026-10-12, after an independent review):** spaCy's tags filed "der" under adverbs, predicative adjectives (wichtig, krank) under adverbs and split modal verbs; about 17 rows were duplicates (müssen x4, alle, welcher ...) and some lemmas joined different words (fahren + führen, wer + was, sich + mich, sein + seine). Now spaCy only separates function from content words, the word type comes from the translator (which sees the sentence), a short hand list fixes wrong lemmas, feminine merging covers Ärztin/Auswanderin/Gerichtsschöffin, and rows the translator gives the same dictionary form are merged. Translations are keyed by the pre-merge rows (data/vocabulary_raw.json) so the merge is stable across rebuilds (it flip-flopped before), and hand fixes moved to data/vocabulary_en_overrides.json (20 entries) so rebuilds keep them. Result: 1,392 words (816 nouns, 246 verbs, 178 adjectives, 67 adverbs, 85 function words), 864 in questions or correct answers; a test now fails on any duplicate dictionary form. Re-translation with word types: $0.093.

## 13. Split every question into "memorize" or "common sense" by hand, and show exam points per lesson

- **Date / status:** 2026-10-05, active, revisited 2026-10-05 after an independent review (see Revision below) (Hayk asked how many questions and points each lesson is worth, and how many are really worth memorizing)
- **Why:** To spend study time where it pays. A question is **common sense** when all three wrong options are absurd or undemocratic (e.g. Q10: fine vs. caning, torture, death penalty), so plain logic or general knowledge picks the answer; it is **memorize** when at least one wrong option is believable (e.g. Q12: "two thirds of the Bundestag can abolish press freedom"). Judged by reading all 310 questions with their options; when in doubt, memorize (the safe side for passing). Result: 156 common sense, 154 memorize. The 154 boil down to 107 facts, because one fact often answers several questions (a lesson fact counts if it brings a memorize question no earlier fact of the lesson covered). **Exam points** per lesson = the expected number of its questions on the sheet: 30/300 per general question, 3/10 per Bayern question, so all lessons add up to 33 (unit-tested). With the existing exam model, common sense alone plus blind guesses averages 20.4 of 33, a 93% pass chance, valid only if the common-sense calls hold and the German is read correctly.
- **Alternatives rejected:** using the pattern-mining rules (PATTERNS.md) as the "easy" label (they are statistical shortcuts about option positions and lengths, not about whether a person can reason to the answer); per-question difficulty from published pass statistics (BAMF publishes none per question); no split (the user asked for it).
- **What would change this:** Practice data showing many wrong answers on common-sense questions (then they are not common sense for this reader: move them to memorize and lower the pass-chance claim).
- **Revision (2026-10-05, same day):** An independent reviewer (fresh-context agent) quoted a believable wrong option for 12 questions I had marked common sense, plus 5 borderline ones. Moved all 12 (42 "die Regierung" makes laws, 93, 104 long illness, 119 the "frei" word games, 208 Gestapo vs Stasi, 212 "Bundesstaat Deutschland", 232 "Europäische Kommission", 241, 254, 258 "Jugendamt pays Kindergeld", 263 "wie Erwachsene", 297 "Polen") and 2 borderline (114 "Pflicht", 23 "Familienunternehmen") to memorize; kept 122, 62, 181 because the question's own wording points to the answer. Numbers before -> after: common sense 156 -> 142, memorize 154 -> 168, facts 107 -> 118, common sense alone 20.4 -> 19.4 of 33, pass chance 93% -> 85%. The facts of moved questions now name their believable wrong option. The UI greys individual question numbers (a fact is grey only when all its questions are common sense, which hid 36 common-sense questions).

## 10. "Known" = the last two answers to a question were right; the pass chance counts only known questions as sure

- **Date / status:** 2026-10-03, active
- **Why:** One right answer is weak evidence: in practice the right answer was often just shown, and a blind guess is right 25% of the time. Two right answers in a row is the simplest rule that filters both, and it is easy to explain on screen. The pass chance then uses the exact exam model from `mine_patterns.py` with two kinds of question (known = always right, everything else = guessed at 25%), ported to `app/src/lib/exam.ts` and unit-tested against independent Python values (0.0009509599956171568 for nothing known, 0.9360898856228623 for 150 + 5 known; matches to 10 decimals). Conservative on purpose: "learning" questions you would often get right count as guesses.
- **Alternatives rejected:** last answer only (counts lucky guesses and just-seen answers as known); a per-question probability from the whole history (needs a Poisson-binomial over draws without replacement, and is harder to explain); FSRS retrievability (spaced repetition was not chosen for this app).
- **What would change this:** Mock exam scores running clearly above the predicted expected score (then the rule is too strict).

## 6. Pattern analysis: in-sample numbers are the headline, cross-validation is the honesty check

- **Date / status:** 2026-10-02, active
- **Why:** The exam is drawn from exactly these 310 questions, so a rule only has to work on them; in-sample precision is what you get on test day. But with 41 rules + 1,456 conditional rules + every word tested, many good-looking numbers are luck, so every learned or mined component is also re-fit on 4/5 and scored on the held-out 1/5 (seed 509), and rule verdicts use Bonferroni. Mined rules (key numbers, word lists) come from the answers themselves and are excluded from the cross-validated rule list. The ranking metric for usefulness is net gain = hits - misses (the misses are exceptions you would have to remember).
- **Measured:** rule list 40% in-sample vs 30% held-out; naive Bayes words+features 56% train vs 39% held-out; conditional rules 34% held-out.
- **Alternatives rejected:** CV-only reporting (understates what the rules give on this closed pool); in-sample-only (would sell overfit rules like "question has 'bayern' -> odd_length 4/5" as real).
- **What would change this:** A new BAMF catalog - then held-out numbers become the relevant ones for the changed questions.

## 5. Exam pass probability computed exactly, not by Monte Carlo

- **Date / status:** 2026-10-02, active
- **Why:** With per-question outcomes in {right, wrong, guess at 25%}, P(pass) is an exact sum over multivariate hypergeometric draws (30/300 general, 3/10 Bayern) times a binomial tail - deterministic, ~ms per evaluation, which makes the 311-point memorization curves cheap. Verified against a 20,000-draw simulation: 0.0071 vs 0.0069 (always B), 0.9361 vs 0.9354 (half memorized).
- **Alternatives rejected:** Monte Carlo (noisy, slower, and 311 curve points x many draws is heavy on this laptop).
- **What would change this:** Strategies with per-question guess probabilities other than 25% (e.g. after crossing out options) - those need a Poisson-binomial or simulation.
