09 Gerichte, Richter, Polizei

Courts, judges, police · Lesson and all exam questions

13 questions · about 1.3 of your 33 exam questions come from this topic · 8 to memorize, 5 common sense

In German courts, ordinary citizens sometimes sit next to the judges, as Schöffen.

In 60 secondsCourtsJudgesOther rolesAll 13 exam questions of this topic
In 60 seconds
  • The Bundesverfassungsgericht interprets the Grundgesetz.
  • Judges belong to the Judikative (rechtsprechende Gewalt); their job is to deliver verdicts.
  • Schöffen are volunteer judges; a Rechtsanwalt advises and represents you.
  • Criminal responsibility starts at 14.
Courts
  • The Bundesverfassungsgericht (Federal Constitutional Court, in Karlsruhe) interprets the Grundgesetz. common sense Q80
  • A court case is a Prozess. There is a criminal trial when someone has committed a crime and is charged. Q146 Q139
Judges
  • A judge works at a court and delivers verdicts: Recht sprechen. common sense Q142 Q147
  • Judges belong to the Judikative, the rechtsprechende Gewalt (judicial power). Q143 Q144 Q145
Other roles
  • Schöffen are volunteer judges (ehrenamtliche Richter) who decide guilt and punishment together with the professional judges. Q140 Q150
  • A Rechtsanwalt (lawyer) advises on legal questions and represents people in court. Q141
  • The police watch that laws are obeyed. common sense Q148
  • Young people are strafmündig from 14: from that age they are punished for crimes, under youth criminal law, so not like adults. Q263
