06 Wahlen: Grundsätze und Wahlrecht

Elections: principles and the right to vote · Lesson and all exam questions

14 questions · about 1.4 of your 33 exam questions come from this topic · 7 to memorize, 7 common sense

German elections run on three words you will see again and again: frei, gleich, geheim.

In 60 seconds
  • Elections are free, equal and secret (frei, gleich, geheim).
  • The people elect the Bundestag; voters must be German citizens aged 18+.
  • Voting is a right, not a duty. 'Aktives Wahlrecht' = you may vote.
  • The party with the most votes wins; regular elections make a change of government possible.
Who votes for what
  • The eligible people (das wahlberechtigte Volk) elect the members of the Bundestag. The Bundestag is the body the people elect, and the Bundestag election is the election of the parliament for Germany. Q28 Q129 Q124
  • You may vote in a Bundestag election if you are a German citizen and at least 18. Q108 Q94
A right, not a duty
  • Taking part in elections is a right (ein Recht). Nobody has to vote. Q114
  • Aktives Wahlrecht = being allowed to vote. (Passives Wahlrecht would be the right to be elected.) Q115 Q116
The principles
  • Frei (free): nobody may influence or force you, a vote brings you no disadvantage, and you decide without pressure whether and whom you vote for. Q5 Q119
  • Elections are geheim (secret); the full formula is frei, gleich, geheim. common sense Q112 Q122
Results
  • The party that gets the most votes wins. common sense Q113
  • Regular elections let the majority of voters change the government. common sense Q125
Watch out
  • Two questions ask 'Wahlen sind frei. Was bedeutet das?' The answer is always about no pressure and no influence. Wrong: 'everyone must vote', 'you may take money for your vote', and the word games on 'frei': choosing where you vote, or your employer giving you time off (frei geben). Q5 Q119
