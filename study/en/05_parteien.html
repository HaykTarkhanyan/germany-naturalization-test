05 Parteien und Koalitionen

Parties and coalitions · Lesson and all exam questions

10 questions · about 1.0 of your 33 exam questions come from this topic · 7 to memorize, 3 common sense

Germany is almost always governed by coalitions, and they get nicknames from the party colours, like the 'Ampel' (traffic light).

In 60 seconds
  • CDU, CSU, SPD, FDP: know what the letters stand for.
  • Parties working together to form a government = Koalition.
  • The two biggest groups in today's Bundestag: CDU/CSU and AfD.
  • A party that fights the constitution can be banned.
The abbreviations
  • CDU = Christlich Demokratische Union. CSU = Christlich Soziale Union, the CDU's sister party that only runs in Bayern. Q76 Q92
  • SPD = Sozialdemokratische Partei Deutschlands. FDP = Freie Demokratische Partei. Q78 Q79
Today's Bundestag
  • The two largest parliamentary groups (Fraktionen) are currently CDU/CSU and AfD (since the February 2025 election). Q73
Coalitions
  • Parties working together to form a government = Koalition. Q31
  • Ampelkoalition (traffic light) = SPD (red), FDP (yellow) and Bündnis 90/Die Grünen (green). Q103
  • Why more than one party? So that the different opinions of citizens are represented. common sense Q41
Banned parties
  • A party aiming for a dictatorship is verfassungswidrig (unconstitutional). A party can be banned when it fights against the constitution, not for criticising the government. common sense Q20 Q43
