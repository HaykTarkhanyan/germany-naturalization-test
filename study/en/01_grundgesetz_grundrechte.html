01 Grundgesetz und Grundrechte

Constitution and basic rights · Lesson and all exam questions

16 questions · about 1.6 of your 33 exam questions come from this topic · 7 to memorize, 9 common sense

Germany wrote its constitution in 1949 so that a dictatorship could never happen again. That is why human dignity comes first, in Article 1.

In 60 seconds
  • The constitution is the Grundgesetz (Basic Law).
  • Article 1: human dignity is untouchable (Menschenwürde).
  • Basic rights include freedom of opinion, of faith and conscience, choosing where you live, and the privacy of letters.
  • Basic rights can't be voted away, not even by a party in the Bundestag.
The Basic Law
  • The German constitution is called the Grundgesetz (Basic Law), in force since 1949. Q6 Q11
  • Article 1 guarantees the inviolability of human dignity (die Unantastbarkeit der Menschenwürde): 'Die Würde des Menschen ist unantastbar.' Q18
Your basic rights (Grundrechte)
  • Freedom of opinion (Meinungsfreiheit) is a basic right. It is why people may openly criticise the government, and it covers saying your opinion on the internet. common sense Q4 Q1 Q14
  • Freedom of faith and conscience (Glaubens- und Gewissensfreiheit) is guaranteed too. Q7
  • Freizügigkeit = you may choose yourself where you live. Q19
  • Opening someone else's letter on purpose breaks the Briefgeheimnis (privacy of correspondence). Q274
  • One basic right is only for foreigners: asylum (Asyl) for people persecuted for political reasons. common sense Q9
Limits and protections
  • Freedom of opinion has a limit: you may not publicly spread false claims about a specific person. common sense Q16
  • Press freedom is a basic right, so a party in the Bundestag cannot abolish it. Q12
  • The Grundgesetz bans forced labour (Zwangsarbeit), and the state may not treat citizens unequally (Ungleichbehandlung). common sense Q15 Q17
  • Punishments must respect dignity: a fine (Geldstrafe) is allowed; beating, torture and the death penalty are not. common sense Q10
Watch out
  • 'Everyone should have the same amount of money' is not in the Grundgesetz. The real principles next to it (dignity, free speech, equality before the law) are. Q8
