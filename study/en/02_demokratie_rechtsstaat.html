02 Demokratie, Rechtsstaat, Gewaltenteilung

Democracy, rule of law, separation of powers · Lesson and all exam questions

15 questions · about 1.5 of your 33 exam questions come from this topic · 5 to memorize, 10 common sense

Four words describe the whole German system: republic, democracy, federal state, rule of law.

In 60 seconds
  • Germany is a republic (not a monarchy) and a democratic and social federal state (Bundesstaat).
  • Volkssouveränität: all state power comes from the people.
  • Rechtsstaat: everyone, including the state, must obey the law.
  • Power is split three ways: Legislative, Executive, Judiciary. The press is not one of them.
What kind of state
  • Germany is a Republik (republic), so it is not a monarchy. common sense Q22 Q34
  • The official formula: Germany is 'ein demokratischer und sozialer Bundesstaat' (a democratic and social federal state). The look-alike 'sozialer und sozialistischer Bundesstaat' is wrong: Germany is not socialist. In the short versions, pick Bundesstaat (not Staatenbund, Staatenverbund or Zentralstaat). Q26 Q27 Q38
Power to the people
  • Volkssouveränität means 'Alle Staatsgewalt geht vom Volke aus': all state power comes from the people. common sense Q52 Q61
Rule of law (Rechtsstaat)
  • Rechtsstaat = all residents and the state itself must obey the laws. common sense Q3 Q53
  • Not compatible with a Rechtsstaat: people being arrested by a private police force without reason. common sense Q51
  • A democracy has a free press, so press censorship (Pressezensur) is not a feature of German democracy. common sense Q30
Separation of powers (Gewaltenteilung)
  • Three powers: Legislative (makes laws: Bundestag and Bundesrat), Exekutive (government, administration, police) and Judikative (the courts). Q60 Q63
  • The press is sometimes called the 'fourth power', but it is not a state power. 'Direktive' is not a power at all. Q32 Q54
Watch out
  • 'Was gehört nicht zur Exekutive?' The answer is the courts: they are the Judikative. Q63
